blurb: Compare the output, then decide.

lead: >
  The BBC is a public body funded largely by a licence fee, and its editorial choices are shaped by that funding model and by its own charter. So is everyone else's.
claim: >
  Two claims need separating here. The first is that the BBC is 'controlled propaganda'. The BBC is regulated by Ofcom and bound by Royal Charter and a written set of editorial guidelines, and it is not owned by commercial shareholders — that is a genuine structural fact, and it is a different thing from being an organ of government. The second is that it systematically promotes a political agenda. The BBC has documented instances of bias, institutional capture and political appointment in its leadership, and serious criticism of its output has come from across the political spectrum, including from people who otherwise support its funding model. The honest version is that every major broadcaster has editorial bias and institutional incentives, and the useful response is media literacy and comparison rather than total rejection of one outlet in favour of another.
alternatives: >
  Watch a genuinely mixed selection of news — broadcasters with different funding models and different editorial outlooks. Check original documents rather than clips. Support the outlets you rely on so the market for serious journalism survives.
responsible: >
  The BBC Board and its editorial leadership set output. Ofcom regulates broadcasting standards and impartiality. Government sets the charter and the licence fee. Individual producers and commissioning editors make daily editorial calls.
demand: >
  More transparency about the editorial process and the influence of funders. Clearer separation between news, opinion and advertising. Stronger accountability for the leadership appointments that critics have repeatedly questioned.
status: >
  Partly. The BBC has published reforms on impartiality and editorial standards, and Ofcom has its own framework for enforcing them. Many of the structural arguments around appointments and funding have not gone away.
sources: >
  Ofcom's Broadcasting Code, the BBC's Editorial Guidelines and Annual Report, and the Royal Charter.
advice: >
  No health issue here — this is a judgement about media. Worth checking the original documentary rather than the summary of it.
