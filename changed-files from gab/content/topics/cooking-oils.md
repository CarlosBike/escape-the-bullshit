title: Cooking Oils
navTitle: Cooking Oils
blurb: Read the label. Check the claim.
order: 4
lead: >
  The category 'cooking oil' covers a wide range of products with very different properties and very different evidence behind them.
claim: >
  The strongest general advice from mainstream nutrition bodies is the opposite of the viral claim: oils high in unsaturated fats, such as olive, rapeseed and sunflower oils, are recommended as part of a healthy diet, and they should replace saturated fats such as butter, lard and coconut oil rather than be added to them. Partially hydrogenated oils and repeated high-temperature heating are worth avoiding, because they are unstable and degrade. 'Processed' is not a meaningful category — refining affects safety and shelf life, not nutritional quality. The useful habit is to compare the label, choose oils high in unsaturated fat, keep them away from excessive heat, and not overheat or reuse oil repeatedly.
alternatives: >
  Olive oil and rapeseed oil for everyday use. Butter in sensible quantities is fine for most people. Store oils away from heat and light, and follow the label's best-before date.
responsible: >
  Manufacturers decide the formulation and the labelling. Public health bodies publish the dietary guidance. Retailers and caterers decide what they use and how often they replace it.
demand: >
  Clearer labelling of oil types and processing methods. Less misleading marketing about 'natural' versus 'processed'. Better fryer-oil practice in catering, with oil changed at documented intervals.
status: >
  Partly. Public health guidance on replacing saturated fats with unsaturated oils is well established and widely followed. Some of the strongest online claims about seed oils and cancer are not supported by the evidence base.
sources: >
  The NHS Eatwell guidance, the Scientific Advisory Committee on Nutrition, and the Cochrane review on dietary fat.
advice: >
  For a specific medical condition — cholesterol, diabetes, pancreatitis — get advice from a doctor or registered dietitian rather than from a list.
