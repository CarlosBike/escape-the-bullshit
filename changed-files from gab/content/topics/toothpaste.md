blurb: Read the ingredients on the label.
order: 3
lead: >
  The label on a toothpaste tube tells you what is in it. That is a better starting point than any brand reputation.
claim: >
  Most mass-market toothpastes contain fluoride, which is added deliberately to help prevent tooth decay and is supported by a large body of dental evidence. Some also contain sodium lauryl sulfate, an effective foaming agent that some people find irritating, or carrageenan, a thickener. The reasonable response is to read the label, check the ingredients of what you currently use, and decide for yourself whether the trade-offs suit you. What you should not do is treat any single ingredient as a proven cause of a serious illness without evidence — that is where the 'poison toothpaste' narrative goes badly wrong.
alternatives: >
  Any toothpaste with fluoride and a reputable dentist's approval is a sound default. If you prefer to avoid certain ingredients, read the label and check with a dentist. Whitening pastes can be abrasive; sensitive teeth usually need a gentler formulation rather than a harsher one.
responsible: >
  Manufacturers choose the formulations and put them on the label. Regulators set the rules on what may be used and how it must be declared. Dentists advise on individual needs.
demand: >
  Clearer labelling, and access to genuinely fluoride-free options that are also not more abrasive. Consumers should be able to compare formulations without marketing language getting in the way.
status: >
  Partly. Fluoride toothpastes at the recommended concentration remain the standard recommendation from dental bodies worldwide, and the evidence base for their safety at those levels is extensive.
sources: >
  The NHS and British Dental Journal on fluoride, and the product ingredient labels themselves.
advice: >
  This one genuinely needs a dentist rather than a website. Check with yours before changing what you use daily.
