lead: >
  A credit score is a summary built by a credit reference agency from data that other lenders report about you.
claim: >
  The three UK credit reference agencies — Experian, Equifax and TransUnion — build your file from information supplied by the companies you hold credit with, plus public record data such as court judgments and bankruptcy notices. The score is a prediction, not a verdict, and the same file can produce different scores at different agencies because the underlying data and the models differ. The practical point is that you are entitled to see your file, check it for errors, and challenge anything wrong; a corrected file is what actually changes your score, not the number itself. You also have a right to a statutory notice of correction. And a credit file is not the same as a record of your character: if you have never borrowed, you may have little or no file at all, which lenders can read in different ways.
alternatives: >
  Check your file with all three agencies, correct errors, and use a credit-building product with a realistic cost if you are starting from nothing. Compare deals by APR rather than headline rate, and check whether a company is authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority.
responsible: >
  The credit reference agencies compile the files. The Financial Conduct Authority authorises and supervises lenders. The Information Commissioner's Office regulates how the agencies handle your data.
demand: >
  Better visibility of what is on your file, simpler and cheaper ways to correct errors, and serious enforcement of the requirement that lenders report accurately and act on disputed information.
status: >
  Partly. Since the 2023 Consumer Duty, lenders are required to treat borrowers fairly, and the FCA has repeatedly pursued firms for misreporting. The cost of checking and correcting files is still high relative to the benefit, and complaints remain slow.
sources: >
  FCA Consumer Duty guidance, the ICO, and the three credit reference agencies' own dispute and correction processes.
advice: >
  If you are dealing with debt or a court application, speak to a free debt adviser — StepChange, Citizens Advice or the National Debtline — rather than acting on a general page like this one.
