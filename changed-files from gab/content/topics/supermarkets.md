lead: >
  The supermarket sector is more scrutinised than it was, and there is a lot of public evidence on how it operates.
claim: >
  UK supermarkets have historically been criticised for their treatment of suppliers, for their role in food waste, and for the way loyalty and payment data is used to influence what people buy. Several supermarket groups have faced competition authority investigations, and the government has run several reviews of the sector's supply practices and the treatment of farmers. Calling the whole sector a monopoly is not accurate — there are several large groups and a long tail of independents — but the concentration of buying power is real and well documented. If you want to change something, the leverage is in choosing where you spend, and in supporting the small shops that are already competing.
alternatives: >
  Shop local where it is practical, use discount grocers, buy loose produce to cut waste, plan meals around what needs using up, and check your loyalty app settings to see what is being tracked.
responsible: >
  The supermarket groups set their own policies and negotiate with suppliers. The Competition and Markets Authority investigates market conduct. The government has reviewed the sector's supply relationships.
demand: >
  Fairer contracts for suppliers, mandatory reporting on food waste, and genuine transparency over loyalty data and pricing algorithms.
status: >
  Partly. The government's groceries code and supply chain reviews have produced real changes, and several competition investigations have resulted in commitments. Supermarkets have also reduced some store waste and expanded discount ranges. Concentration remains high.
sources: >
  The CMA's groceries market investigation, the DEF groceries code review, and Which? and Kantar retail data.
advice: >
  Nothing medical here — this is a spending decision. Keep receipts if you ever need to raise a specific complaint.
