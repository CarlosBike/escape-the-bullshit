navTitle: Amalgam Fillings
blurb: Ask your dentist about the options.
order: 9
lead: >
  Dental amalgam is a genuinely contested material, and the debate is about risk management and regulation rather than about a secret.
claim: >
  Amalgam fillings contain elemental mercury bound into an alloy with silver, tin and copper. It is a durable, inexpensive filling material with a long clinical record, and the mercury is released as a small amount of vapour that rises above the filling. The regulatory position has moved: the EU's 2017 regulation restricted mercury amalgam use, with exemptions for some products, and the FDA in the US has recommended that certain high-risk groups — notably pregnant people and children — avoid it where alternatives are available. The evidence on harm to the general population at normal exposure levels has not shown a clear effect, and the mainstream dental position remains that amalgam is safe for most people. Removing a sound amalgam filling solely to remove mercury releases a large dose of mercury vapour at the moment of drilling, which is why the established guidance advises against routine removal.
alternatives: >
  Ask your dentist what filling material is appropriate for the tooth and your circumstances. If you want to avoid amalgam in future, request a resin-based or glass-ionomer alternative. If you are pregnant, planning a pregnancy, or the filling is on a child's tooth, mention it when you book.
responsible: >
  Dentists choose the material for each tooth. The General Dental Council sets the professional standards. The Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency authorises products, and the regulators in each country set the restrictions on use.
demand: >
  Clearer disclosure of what material is used and what the alternatives are. Patient choice on mercury-free care where clinically appropriate. Better long-term evidence on exposure.
status: >
  Partly. Several countries have restricted or phased out amalgam use, with exemptions varying by product. The UK's position still permits it in general practice, and the guidance on not removing sound amalgam fillings remains in place.
sources: >
  The FDA's recommendation on dental amalgam, the EU Regulation 2017/852, and the GDC's guidance on safe use.
advice: >
  This one definitely needs a dentist. Do not have a filling removed on the strength of a website page alone.
