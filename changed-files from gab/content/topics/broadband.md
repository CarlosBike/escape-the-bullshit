title: Broadband
navTitle: Broadband
blurb: Stop paying for a fixed broadband line you don't need.
order: 2
featured: true
lead: >
  A fixed broadband contract is an easy expense to overlook if you barely use it. You do not need one for most household internet.
claim: >
  Most people use a tiny fraction of the data their fixed line provides. If your usage is light, a phone SIM with a generous allowance, plus a router or access point you already own, will cover ordinary browsing, video calls and streaming on a few devices. The real cost is not always the monthly price — it is also the 18-month term, the early exit charges, and the fact that you cannot get a phone signal indoors. Comparing what you actually use against the cheapest fixed plan is usually the fastest saving on the household bills.
alternatives: >
  Use a phone connection with tethering or a mobile broadband device if your usage is light and the signal is good where you live. Keep a fixed line if you work from home, stream in 4K, or have several people streaming at once. Read the actual speed and download limits before switching.
responsible: >
  Broadband providers set the price, terms and minimum contract length. Ofgem regulates the market and the switching process.
demand: >
  Make short-term and rolling contracts the normal offer. Ban or reduce minimum terms and early exit charges. Make switching genuinely simple and quick.
status: >
  Partly. Switching has become much easier since the automatic switching system was introduced, and a 'switch to stop' regime allows existing customers to cut off a service and request cancellation of the automatic switch. Minimum contract terms remain a common complaint.
sources: >
  Ofgem's broadband switching pages and Citizens Advice guidance.
advice: >
  Measure your real monthly usage before cancelling anything.
