navTitle: Soya Milk
blurb: Check the ingredients and the nutrition label.
order: 11
lead: >
  Soya milk is one of the most evidence-backed of the plant milks. The loudest claims against it are the weakest part of the argument.
claim: >
  Soya is a complete protein and contains isoflavones, which are a type of phyto-oestrogen. A great deal of research has looked at whether these affect hormone-related outcomes in adults, and the large body of evidence has not found meaningful harm at normal dietary amounts; some studies have found neutral or beneficial results. The claim that soya causes feminisation or widespread disease in the general population is not supported by that evidence. The practical questions are more ordinary: which varieties contain sugar, which are fortified with calcium and vitamin D, and whether you personally feel better on one than another. Fortification matters more than most people realise — an unfortified soya drink can be a poor substitute for dairy in terms of calcium and iodine.
alternatives: >
  Choose a fortified soya drink if you use one as a dairy substitute, particularly for calcium and vitamin D. Compare added sugars across the range. If you prefer alternatives, oat and unsweetened nut drinks are also widely available, and the environmental and nutrition arguments between them are genuinely mixed rather than settled.
responsible: >
  Manufacturers decide the formulation and fortification. Public health bodies publish dietary guidance. Retailers decide what they stock.
demand: >
  Clearer labelling of added sugars and fortification. More fortified products at affordable prices. Continued funding of independent nutrition research.
status: >
  Partly. Guidance from major health bodies consistently identifies soya foods as safe and potentially beneficial as part of a balanced diet, and the regulatory position has stayed broadly stable.
sources: >
  The Scientific Advisory Committee on Nutrition's report on soya, the British Dietetic Association, and the NHS Eatwell guidance.
advice: >
  If you have a hormone-sensitive condition, are pregnant, or are feeding a baby, talk to a doctor or a registered dietitian about your own circumstances.
