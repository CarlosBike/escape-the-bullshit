blurb: Understand what each one costs you.

featured: true
lead: >
  Cash and card are not identical products. One preserves your privacy, the other preserves your time.
claim: >
  Cash is the more private option and the only one that many small traders can afford to accept without paying a card fee. Card payments are faster, more convenient and increasingly the only option in some places, and they are protected by strong consumer fraud rules — Chargeback and Section 75 give you a route to get money back when something goes wrong, which is why a blanket 'always use cash' rule can leave you worse off. The genuine costs to argue about are the card fees that small businesses pay and pass on, and the fact that digital payments make your spending easier to track. The UK has already legislated to end the fees that large chains charge for card acceptance, with the 'Project Darwin' work reducing them for smaller retailers.
alternatives: >
  Use cash where you reasonably can and where the business prefers it, and keep a card for convenience and for the fraud protection. Set spending alerts on your account if tracking is a concern. Use a privacy-conscious card if you want less data attached to your spending.
responsible: >
  The card networks and issuing banks set the fees and run the payment system. The government legislates on what businesses can charge for card payments. The Financial Conduct Authority and Payment Systems Regulator oversee the system.
demand: >
  Lower or no card fees for small businesses. Stronger privacy defaults and less data attached to individual transactions. Accessible cash provision for people who need it.
status: >
  Partly. The restriction on surcharging for card payments has materially reduced the cost, and work to reduce merchant fees has continued. Privacy defaults and the cash infrastructure remain live arguments.
sources: >
  The Payment Systems Regulator, the government's merchant and acquirer fees reforms, and the Financial Conduct Authority's fraud guidance.
advice: >
  If you are actually trying to recover money from a fraudulent card payment, talk to your bank first and keep the transaction records.
