title: Location on Your Phone
navTitle: Location on Your Phone
blurb: Switch it off when you do not need it.
order: 5
lead: >
  Location services are genuinely useful. The question is whether they are on all the time, and who gets the data.
claim: >
  Most phones let you grant location access per app rather than globally, and the useful habit is to check that list every few months. Apps for maps, weather and photography legitimately need location while in use; many others have no business knowing where you are at all, and some background location permissions are granted without you realising it. Location data is also collected from cell towers and Wi-Fi networks regardless of the phone setting, which is why 'switch it off' stops one kind of tracking rather than all of it. Revoking permissions does not generally break anything, because you can grant them again in seconds.
alternatives: >
  Set location to 'while using the app' rather than 'always', review which apps hold permission, and revoke the ones you do not recognise. Use a privacy-respecting browser and search engine, and turn off advertising identifiers where your platform offers that option.
responsible: >
  The app developers request the permissions. The platform decides what the options mean. Search and advertising companies buy and combine location-derived data. Regulators set the legal rules on consent and sale.
demand: >
  Permissions that are off by default for anything that is not essential, plain-language explanations before a permission prompt, and a proper audit trail for location data brokers.
status: >
  Partly. Regulation in the UK and EU has strengthened considerably, including rules on location data and advertising identifiers, and major platforms now offer controls to disable some of it. Enforcement and transparency are still incomplete.
sources: >
  Apple and Google privacy documentation, and the UK Information Commissioner's guidance on location data.
advice: >
  Turn off location for anything that is not a map, a camera or a transport app. It costs nothing and you can undo it.
