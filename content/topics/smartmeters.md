lead: >
  Smart meters are optional in the UK, and the data they collect is more detailed than a traditional meter ever was.
claim: >
  Smart meters send frequent, detailed readings of how you use energy. That is useful for spotting waste, but it also creates a record of your household routines. They are supplied and billed for like any other part of your energy, and many people find the projected-versus-actual estimates on the bill more annoying than useful. The more useful question is what the extra data is actually used for, and who gets to see it.
alternatives: >
  Ask your supplier for a traditional meter where one is offered, or request that half-hourly readings are switched off if you do not want near-real-time data. Switching off the data stream does not affect your tariff or your bill.
responsible: >
  The energy suppliers install and operate the meters. Government policy and the regulator decide what data may be collected and shared, and how long it is kept.
demand: >
  Make opting in genuinely informed and reversible. Publish clearly how often readings are taken, who receives them, and how long they are retained. Make traditional meters a straightforward request rather than a fight.
status: >
  Not fully. The Data Use and Access Act 2025 moves smart-meter data governance towards a new body and introduces more consumer choice, but much of the detail is still being worked out, and there have been significant delays in rolling it out.
sources: >
  Ofgem (smart meters and smart meter data) and the UK Government's Smart Meter and Data Access consultation material.
advice: >
  This is about your energy data and billing, not a medical matter — but check your actual tariff terms before switching anything off.
